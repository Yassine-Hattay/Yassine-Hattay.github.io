🧠 Thesis Insights & Pareto Front Analysis

💡 The Quantization Format Paradox (Legacy x86)
Thesis Finding: On legacy CPUs lacking AVX-512/VNNI (e.g., Pentium G5420), smaller K-quant formats like Q3_K_M or Q4_K_M are actually slower than the older, larger Q4_0 format. The complex bitwise unpacking required for K-quants bottlenecks the CPU. Q4_0 maintains ~98.8% accuracy relative to F16 and is the strictly Pareto-dominant choice for older hardware.
🧠 Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) on Edge Devices
Thesis Finding: On GPUs, MoE is a capacity play. On CPUs, MoE is a bandwidth play. Because generation is memory-bandwidth-bound, an MoE model (e.g., 14B total, 2.7B active) only streams the active experts per token. This drastically reduces B_total in the Roofline equation, allowing MoE models to bypass the sub-1 tok/s bottleneck that traps dense 7B models on bandwidth-starved hardware.
📉 Flash Attention & KV Cache on CPU
Thesis Finding: Flash Attention (-fa 1) is designed for GPU HBM/SRAM hierarchies. On CPU, the L3 cache handles attention state differently. Empirical sweeps showed Flash Attention is neutral at short context, but causes a 3× performance regression at 15K context depths on CPU. Always use -fa 0. Similarly, KV-cache quantization is a net loss on compute-rich CPUs (dequantization costs more cycles than the bandwidth saved), but flips to a net win on ultra-low bandwidth edge devices.
